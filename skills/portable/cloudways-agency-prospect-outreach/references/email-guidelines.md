# Writing the outreach draft

The reader is a busy business owner who did not ask to hear from you. The email has about five seconds to prove it was written for them.

## Structure (under 120 words)

1. **Opener tied to them.** One line showing you looked at their business specifically. No "I hope this finds you well".
2. **One or two findings, in business language.** Taken straight from the audit. Include the measured number when there is one.
3. **Why it matters.** One sentence connecting the finding to lost customers, calls, bookings or trust.
4. **Credibility (optional).** One line about a similar project from the profile, only if the profile has one. Never invent a client or result.
5. **One easy ask.** Offer something useful with low commitment: "Want me to send the full audit?" or "Worth a 15 minute call next week?" Pick one, not both.
6. **Signature and footer.** Sender name, agency, business address, and a one line opt-out such as "Not relevant? Reply 'no' and I won't email again."

## Subject line

Short, specific, lowercase is fine. Reference their business or the finding.

- Good: "quick note on smithdental.co.uk on mobile"
- Good: "your site's security certificate"
- Avoid: "Grow your business 10x!", "Partnership opportunity", fake "Re:" or "Fwd:"

## Tone

Helpful peer, not salesperson. Plain words. No exclamation marks, no buzzwords ("synergy", "cutting edge", "digital transformation"), no false urgency, no flattery that could apply to anyone.

## Example

Subject: smithdental.co.uk on mobile

Hi Sarah,

I was looking at dentists in Leeds and opened Smith Dental on my phone. The homepage took 7.8 seconds to load and shows at desktop size, so the "Book now" button is hard to tap. Most people searching for a dentist do it on a phone and give up after a few seconds.

We rebuilt the site for Park Lane Orthodontics last year and their online bookings went up by a third.

Want me to send over the full audit? It's free and takes two minutes to read.

Ali Khan
Brightline Web, 12 King Street, Leeds LS1 2HL
Not relevant? Reply "no" and I won't email again.

## Before saving each draft, check

- Every factual claim matches the audit output
- The recipient's name and business name are spelled exactly as on their site
- Under 120 words in the body
- Footer has address and opt-out
- Email status (verified or guessed) is noted for the user
